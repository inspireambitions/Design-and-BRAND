# Muqabala product and writing context

## Audience

Muqabala serves job seekers preparing for Gulf interviews. Many users speak English as a second or third language. Some have limited formal education. Most will use a phone and may feel nervous about interviews, technology or being judged.

Write for an adult who is capable but busy, worried and unfamiliar with interview language. Never sound childish or talk down to the reader.

## Main action

Help the user start a free practice interview at `/practice`.

## Core promise

Choose a job. Speak or type an answer. See what worked, what is missing and what to add next.

## Voice

- Use British English.
- Use short, active sentences.
- Put the important point first.
- Use familiar words.
- Explain one idea at a time.
- Sound calm, practical and respectful.
- Write Arabic in clear Modern Standard Arabic with the same simple meaning.
- Never use an em dash.

## Plain-language rules

- Aim for words a young secondary-school reader can understand.
- Keep most sentences below 15 words.
- Use numbered steps for any process with three or more actions.
- Keep paragraphs to one or two short sentences.
- Use button labels that say what happens next.
- Explain necessary terms the first time they appear.

Prefer:

- `feedback` over `assessment`
- `written words` over `transcript`
- `what you did well` over `evidence found`
- `what to add next` over `areas for improvement`
- `job advert` over `role specification`
- `AI service` over `configured AI provider`
- `we will not give a score` over `the answer will remain unscored`

## Trust rules

State these facts in plain language near important actions:

- Practice is free and needs no sign-up.
- No employer can see the practice.
- Video stays on the user's device.
- Muqabala scores the answer, not the face, accent or personality.
- Users can type and get the same answer feedback.
- AI can make mistakes, so users should check the written words and feedback.

## Blog rules

Every future WordPress article must solve one clear job-seeker problem. Use short sections, numbered steps and real answer examples. Do not publish thin, padded or jargon-heavy articles.
