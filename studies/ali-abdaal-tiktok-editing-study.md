## 1. Overall format

| Element | Typical spec |
|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical (1080×1920) |
| Length | 30–90 seconds |
| Structure | Hook (0–3s) → promise → 2–4 point body → payoff/CTA |
| Framing | Talking head, chest-up, subject slightly below center so captions sit in the safe zone |
| Source footage | Usually cut down from a longer landscape YouTube video, reframed to vertical with punch-ins |

## 2. Pacing and cuts

- **Jump-cut driven.** Every breath, pause, and filler word is cut out. Average
  shot length is roughly 1.5–3 seconds; the audio feels like one continuous,
  dense sentence.
- **Punch-in alternation.** Instead of B-roll for every beat, he alternates
  between two "virtual cameras" from the same take: a wide and a ~110–130%
  digital punch-in. Each cut lands on a sentence boundary or emphasis word,
  which keeps visual rhythm without any extra footage.
- **Hook is re-cut tightest.** The first 3 seconds usually contain the boldest
  claim of the video, often mid-thought ("The reason you're always tired
  is…"), with the caption already on screen at frame one.
- **No transitions.** Hard cuts only. No dissolves, wipes, or zoom-blur
  transitions — the energy comes from cut frequency, not transition effects.

## 3. Captions (the signature graphic element)

Captions are the dominant graphic layer and do most of the "editing" work:

- **Style:** bold, heavy sans-serif (Montserrat ExtraBold / Proxima Nova Bold /
  a similar geometric grotesque), white fill, strong black drop shadow or thin
  dark outline for legibility on any background.
- **Placement:** lower-middle third, centered, 2–4 words per line, one short
  phrase on screen at a time (not full sentences).
- **Word-level timing:** captions appear phrase-by-phrase or word-by-word,
  synced tightly to speech (the "karaoke" style popularized by CapCut and
  Submagic/Captions.ai).
- **Emphasis color:** one keyword per phrase is highlighted — typically
  **yellow** (sometimes green or red for negative words) — chosen for the
  word carrying the point ("money", "focus", "wrong").
- **Micro-animation:** each phrase pops in with a quick scale bounce
  (~105% → 100% over 5–8 frames). Occasionally a subtle shake on high-energy
  words.
- **Emoji:** used sparingly, appended to a phrase (💰, 🧠, ⚡) rather than
  scattered everywhere.

## 4. Supporting graphics

- **Screenshots and cutaways:** when he cites a book, app, or study, a
  full-screen or picture-in-picture cutaway appears for 1–2 seconds — book
  cover, notion screenshot, chart — usually with a slight slow zoom (Ken
  Burns) and a drop shadow over a plain or blurred background.
- **Lists on screen:** numbered points sometimes stack as text lines that stay
  on screen as he progresses ("1. Energy → 2. Focus → 3. Time"), reinforcing
  the structure of the argument.
- **Arrows / circles / underlines:** hand-drawn-style scribble annotations
  animate on to point at things in screenshots. Usually white, red, or yellow, drawn
  on over ~10 frames.
- **B-roll:** minimal and functional — desk shots, book close-ups, screen
  recordings. It supports a specific sentence rather than filling space.

## 5. Color, light, and grade

- **Set:** warm, cozy home-studio look — wood tones, warm practical lamps,
  bookshelves, soft window light. Background is 1–2 stops darker than his
  face, with shallow depth of field (blurred but readable).
- **Grade:** natural, slightly warm, lifted shadows, controlled highlights.
  No teal-orange stylization; skin tones stay realistic. Contrast is gentle —
  the "friendly doctor" look rather than a cinematic grade.
- **Consistency:** the same set and grade across videos acts as a brand
  identity in itself; a viewer can identify his content from a single frame.

## 6. Sound design

- **Music:** quiet lo-fi / soft upbeat instrumental bed, ducked well under the
  voice (roughly −20 dB relative). Never a trending TikTok sound driving the
  edit.
- **Voice:** heavily processed for closeness — compressed, de-essed, slight
  presence boost. Very consistent loudness so it survives phone speakers.
- **SFX:** subtle whooshes/pops on caption hits and cutaway entrances, soft
  "ding" or page-turn on list items. Sparse enough that each one still lands.

## 7. Why it works (principles to steal)

1. **Retention through density, not spectacle.** Cuts remove every dead
   millisecond; the viewer never has a moment to swipe.
2. **Captions are the primary visual.** The video is fully consumable on mute;
   the highlighted keyword tells you what matters even at a glance.
3. **Two-camera illusion from one take.** Punch-in alternation is free B-roll.
4. **One graphic idea at a time.** Each cutaway/list/annotation maps to exactly
   one spoken sentence — graphics never compete with each other.
5. **Brand through consistency.** Same font, same highlight color, same warm
   set, same music genre → recognition without a logo.

## 8. Replication recipe (CapCut / Premiere)

1. Record 4K talking head (allows clean vertical punch-ins from a single take).
2. Rough cut: strip silences and fillers (Timebolt / Premiere's text-based
   editing / CapCut "remove pauses").
3. Alternate 100% and ~120% scale on sentence boundaries.
4. Auto-caption, then restyle: bold sans-serif, white + black shadow, 2–4 words
   per screen, yellow keyword per phrase, pop-in animation, placed lower-middle.
5. Add cutaways only where a specific noun is spoken (book, app, chart), 1–2s
   each with slow zoom.
6. Music bed at low volume, whoosh/pop SFX on caption and cutaway hits.
7. Hook check: bold claim audible AND readable within the first 1.5 seconds.
